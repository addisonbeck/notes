(Metric)

## Ingredients
- 480g all-purpose flour
- 5g baking soda (1 teaspoon)
- 5g salt (1 teaspoon)
- 12g sugar (optional, traditional recipes often omit this)
- 420ml buttermilk
- 30g cold butter (optional)
- 120g raisins or currants (optional)
- 8g caraway seeds (optional)

## Instructions
1. Preheat oven to 425°F (220°C). Prepare a baking sheet by lightly dusting with flour or lining with parchment paper.

2. In a large bowl, sift together the flour, baking soda, and salt. If using sugar, mix it in now.

3. If using butter, cut it into small pieces and work it into the flour mixture with your fingertips until it resembles coarse crumbs.

4. If using raisins and/or caraway seeds, stir them into the dry ingredients.

5. Make a well in the center and pour in the buttermilk. Mix quickly with a fork to form a soft dough. The dough should be somewhat sticky but manageable.

6. Turn out onto a floured surface and knead lightly just enough to shape into a round loaf, about 20cm in diameter. Do not over-knead; this will toughen the bread.

7. Place on the prepared baking sheet. Cut a deep cross on top with a sharp knife (this helps the bread cook through and, traditionally, "lets the fairies out").

8. Bake for 30-35 minutes, until the bread is golden brown and sounds hollow when tapped on the bottom.

9. Transfer to a wire rack and let cool. Traditional Irish soda bread is best eaten fresh, ideally on the same day it's made.

## Notes
- For a more authentic version, omit the sugar, butter, raisins, and caraway seeds.
- The cross cut on top has practical benefits (helps bread cook through) but also holds cultural significance in Ireland.
- Buttermilk can be substituted by mixing 15ml of lemon juice or white vinegar with enough regular milk to make 420ml, then letting it sit for 5-10 minutes.
- In traditional Irish households, soda bread would be cooked in a cast iron pot over an open fire or in a bastible (a type of Dutch oven).
